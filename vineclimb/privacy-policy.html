Privacy Policy for Vineclimb

Effective date: September 13, 2026
Last updated: September 16, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how the Vineclimb mobile app (“Vineclimb”, “the app”, “we”, “us”) handles information. Vineclimb is a daily vine-path puzzle game published by Wadaya Labs (“the developer”).

Summary

  • Vineclimb has no accounts and no login, and the developer operates no server. The app itself makes no network requests to us — we never receive your personal information.
  • Your game progress, statistics, and settings are stored only on your device and are deleted when you clear the app's data or uninstall it.
  • The app shows ads through Google AdMob. To serve ads, Google may collect data such as your device's advertising identifier and IP address. This is the only data collection connected to the app.
  • If you choose to send a challenge to a friend, the app hands a link or code — containing the display name you chose and your result — to whichever app you pick. That happens only when you tap share, and it never passes through us.
  • If you installed Vineclimb from a friend's challenge link, Google Play passes that challenge code back to the app once, on first launch (the Google Play Install Referrer), so the board you were invited to can open straight away. The code contains only the board reference, your friend's display name and their time. It is read on your device and is not sent to us.
  • In the EEA, UK, and Switzerland, you are asked for consent before personalized ads are used, and you can revisit that choice in Settings at any time.

1. Information we collect

Information the developer collects: none

We do not require an account; we do not ask for your name, email address, or contact details; and we do not operate any server that receives data from the app. The app's own code makes no network connections — the only component that communicates over the internet is the Google AdMob SDK described below.

Information stored only on your device

The app saves the following locally on your device. It is never transmitted to us, and it is permanently removed when you clear the app's data or uninstall the app:

  • Game progress and statistics: solved levels, per-board best times and move counts, daily-challenge status, achievements, and streak data (including streak-save records).
  • The display name you type on the welcome screen, or the suggested name the app generates for you if you leave it blank.
  • Your own pace history, used to grade your solves (see “On-device pacing” below).
  • Your preferences: sound, theme, arrow style, direction-help setting, and whether the reminder permission has already been asked for.
  • Your advertising-consent status and ad-personalization preference.
  • Ad-pacing state (for example, when an ad was last shown), used only to limit how often ads appear.

On-device pacing (medals and your “par”)

To award medals and show how a solve compares with your recent form, the app takes the median of your own recent solve times at each grid size, entirely on your device. This is a comparison with your own past play only — it is not a test, a medical or cognitive assessment, or a comparison with other people. It is stored only on your device, is used only to run the game, is never transmitted anywhere, and is deleted with the app's data.

Challenges you choose to share

Vineclimb lets you challenge someone to a board you have solved. When — and only when — you tap a share button, the app builds a link or short code that contains the board, your display name, and your result, and hands it to your device's standard share sheet so you can send it through an app you choose (a messenger, email, the clipboard, and so on). Some share options also attach an image of your result, which the app writes to its own cache folder on your device for that purpose.

There is no server behind this feature. The developer neither sends nor receives any part of it; once you hand the link to another app, that app's own privacy policy governs what happens next. Nothing is shared unless you actively choose to share it.

Information collected by Google AdMob (third party)

Vineclimb displays full-screen interstitial ads and optional rewarded ads using Google AdMob. To deliver, frequency-cap, measure, and (where permitted) personalize ads and to prevent fraud and abuse, Google and its advertising partners may collect and process information such as:

  • A device advertising identifier (e.g., Android Advertising ID).
  • IP address and approximate, IP-derived location (e.g., country or region).
  • Device and app information, and ad-interaction and diagnostic data.

This processing is performed by Google, not by us, and we do not receive this data. Google's handling of it is governed by Google's own policies:

  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services
  • Google Privacy Policy
  • AdMob / Google Mobile Ads

Note on Google Play's Data safety section: Google Play treats data collected by SDKs inside an app as collected by that app. The app's Data safety listing therefore declares the AdMob-related data above (such as advertising identifiers) as collected and shared for advertising purposes, even though the developer never receives it.

2. How information is used

  • On-device data is used only to run the game: saving your progress, remembering your settings, pacing ads, grading your solves, and scheduling reminders — all on your device.
  • AdMob data is used by Google to serve and measure ads, prevent fraud and abuse, and, where you have consented, personalize the ads you see.

We do not sell your personal information for money, and we do not use data for any purpose other than operating the game and displaying ads as described. Serving personalized ads through Google may qualify as “sharing” of identifiers under some laws; see Section 8 for your choices.

3. Advertising and your choices

Consent (EEA, UK, Switzerland)

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, or Switzerland, the app presents a consent request (via Google's User Messaging Platform) before personalized ads are used. You can revisit, change, or withdraw your choice at any time from the app's Settings, which reopens Google's consent options (where you can choose non-personalized ads or withdraw consent). Your consent choice is stored on your device.

Everyone

You can also limit ad personalization at the device level:

  • Android: Settings → Google → Ads — opt out of ad personalization, or reset or delete your advertising ID.

Choosing non-personalized ads or opting out does not remove ads; it changes how they are selected.

4. Reminders (local notifications)

Vineclimb can show a daily reminder (around 7 pm local time, at most once per day, and not on days you have already played). On Android 13 and newer the app asks for notification permission once, the first time it runs; if you decline, no reminder is ever scheduled and you are not asked again. On older Android versions no permission dialog exists, so the reminder is scheduled from the first launch. Reminders are scheduled entirely on your device: there is no push service and nothing is sent to any server. You can turn them off at any time in your device's notification settings (long-press the app icon → Notifications, or Settings → Apps → Vineclimb → Notifications).

5. Security

Your game data never leaves your device, which is the strongest protection available for it. Communication between your device and Google's ad services is encrypted in transit by Google's SDK.

6. Permissions the app uses

  • Internet / network state (INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE) — required by Google AdMob to load ads, and used to tell whether an ad can be requested at all. The app itself makes no other network connections.
  • Vibration (VIBRATE) — short haptic feedback on moves, refusals and solves. No data is involved.
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS, Android 13+) — to show the daily reminder; requested once on first launch, and you may decline.
  • Run at device startup (RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED) — so an already-scheduled reminder survives a restart; nothing else runs at startup. The reminder is scheduled with approximate timing, so the app does not use exact alarms.
  • Advertising ID (com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID) — added by the Google ads SDK to access the advertising identifier described in Section 1.

7. Children

Vineclimb is not directed to children under 13 (or the equivalent minimum age in your country), and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided personal information through the app, contact us at the address below and we will assist; note that because the developer holds no user data, ad-identifier concerns are handled through the device controls in Section 3 and through Google.

8. Your regional rights

EEA / UK (GDPR): Where GDPR applies, the legal bases relevant to the app are consent (for personalized advertising) and legitimate interests (for serving non-personalized ads, frequency capping, and fraud prevention, performed by Google). You have rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, and objection, and the right to withdraw consent at any time without affecting prior processing. For on-device game data you exercise these directly — view it in the app, or erase it by clearing the app's data or uninstalling. For ad data processed by Google, use the controls in Section 3 or contact Google. Ad data may be processed on Google servers outside your country, including in the United States, under Google's safeguards for international transfers. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with your local supervisory authority.

California (CCPA/CPRA): We do not sell personal information for money. Serving personalized ads via Google may constitute “sharing” of identifiers for cross-context behavioral advertising under the CPRA; you can opt out of this using Android's ad settings to opt out of ad personalization or reset your advertising ID (see Section 3). We do not knowingly share the personal information of consumers under 16.

Australia (Privacy Act / APPs): The developer does not collect personal information; ad-related information is handled by Google as described above.
